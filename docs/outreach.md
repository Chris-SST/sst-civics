# Getting Viapunt known: outreach list and message drafts

Written 7 November 2026, as part 4 of `docs/content-plan.md`. Nothing here has been sent: every message goes out from Chris's own address, after he has read it. Contact details are not listed on purpose. Take them from each organisation's own website when sending, so none are guessed.

## When to start

Start once the glossary, the eligibility checker and the Zurich checklist are live, and the first two articles are published (around 23 November 2026). Organisations link to a useful resource, not to a sales page.

## Who to contact, in order of value

| # | Who | What to offer | Why they would link |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Integration offices: the Fachstelle Integration of the canton of Zurich, Integrationsförderung of the city of Zurich and Winterthur, and the integration offices of Aargau, Bern, Basel-Stadt, Lucerne and St Gallen (as each canton guide goes live) | The glossary, the document checklist, and the guides in 8 languages | Their clients ask these questions every day; a free, sourced and independent resource in the clients' languages saves them time |
| 2 | Language schools with B1 and naturalisation courses (for example ECAP, Klubschule Migros, local adult-education centres) | The knowledge-test pages and the free practice questions as course homework | Their course participants are exactly the applicants |
| 3 | Diaspora associations and their social media groups (Portuguese, Albanian and Kosovar, Turkish, Italian, Spanish, North Macedonian, Serbian) | The dual-citizenship article and the guide in their language | Little good content exists in these languages |
| 4 | Expat communities: English Forum Switzerland, r/askswitzerland, the "Expats in Zurich" groups | Answer real questions properly, and link only where a page actually answers it | Long-lived threads keep sending readers |
| 5 | Media: Newly Swissed, swissinfo (Swiss abroad and migration pages), local papers (Tages-Anzeiger Zurich section, Aargauer Zeitung) | Story: "a free, independent guide to naturalisation in eight languages, with sources on every page" | New, useful, local |
| 6 | Libraries and community centres (Pestalozzi-Bibliothek Zurich, GZ community centres) | A printed A4 poster or flyer with a QR code to the checklist | Visible where applicants are |
| 7 | Swiss Science Tutors website | One link from the About page or the footer ("Our naturalisation guide: viapunt.ch") | Same company; an easy first link |

## Forum rules (number 4)

- Use a personal account that says openly that you work on Viapunt.
- Answer the question in full in the thread itself. Link only as "more detail and sources here".
- At most one link per thread, and never post the same text twice.

## Draft: media pitch (English)

> **Subject:** A free naturalisation guide in eight languages, with sources on every page
>
> Hello <name>,
>
> Every year tens of thousands of people in Switzerland apply for citizenship. Much of what they find online is outdated, unsourced or in a language they don't read well. Viapunt (viapunt.ch) is a new, independent guide that explains the process step by step, starting with the canton of Zurich. It covers the knowledge test, the interview, language certificates and fees, cites the official source on every page, and is available in German, English, French, Italian, Portuguese, Spanish, Albanian and Turkish.
>
> If a piece on how people prepare for naturalisation, or on the knowledge test itself, is of interest, I'd be glad to talk or to share figures on what applicants ask most.
>
> Best regards,
> Chris Arrell, Viapunt, info@viapunt.ch
