# Example questions: IB Physics

Style examples for the generator, written as drafts for SST tutor review. Not past-paper
material. One per type. Calculators are allowed in every paper; take
\(g = 9.81\ \text{m s}^{-2}\).

## mcq, difficulty 2, PH-A.2, SL

A 2.0 kg box rests on a horizontal floor. A horizontal force of 12 N acts on it and the box
accelerates at \(4.0\ \text{m s}^{-2}\). What is the magnitude of the friction force?
A. \(0\ \text{N}\)  B. \(4.0\ \text{N}\)  C. \(8.0\ \text{N}\)  D. \(20\ \text{N}\)
Answer: B. Solution: \(F_{\text{net}} = ma = 8.0\ \text{N}\), so friction
\(= 12 - 8.0 = 4.0\ \text{N}\).
Distractors: ignoring friction (A), quoting the net force (C), adding instead of subtracting (D).

## numeric, difficulty 3, PH-A.1, SL

A stone is thrown vertically upwards at \(15\ \text{m s}^{-1}\) from the edge of a cliff and
lands at the bottom 4.2 s later. Find the height of the cliff, in metres, to two significant
figures.
Answer: 24 (m), tolerance 0.03.
Solution: taking up as positive, \(s = ut + \frac{1}{2}at^2 = 15(4.2) - 4.905(4.2)^2 = -23.5\),
so the cliff is about 24 m high.

## short, difficulty 3, PH-B.5, SL

Two resistors of \(6.0\ \Omega\) and \(3.0\ \Omega\) are connected in parallel. State the
combined resistance.
Answer: `2.0 \Omega` (numeric check: 2.0). Solution:
\(\frac{1}{R} = \frac{1}{6} + \frac{1}{3} = \frac{1}{2}\), so \(R = 2.0\ \Omega\).

## structured, difficulty 4, PH-D.1, HL, 6 marks

A satellite of mass 450 kg orbits a planet of mass \(6.0 \times 10^{24}\ \text{kg}\) in a
circular orbit of radius \(8.0 \times 10^{6}\ \text{m}\).
(a) Show that the orbital speed is about \(7.1\ \text{km s}^{-1}\). [3]
(b) Calculate the period of the orbit. [2]
(c) Explain why the satellite's kinetic energy is constant although a force acts on it. [1]
Mark scheme: (a) M1 equating gravitational force to centripetal force; M1
\(v = \sqrt{\frac{GM}{r}}\); A1 \(v = 7.07 \times 10^{3}\ \text{m s}^{-1}\) (one extra
figure shown). (b) M1 \(T = \frac{2\pi r}{v}\); A1 \(7.1 \times 10^{3}\ \text{s}\) (about
2.0 hours). (c) R1 the force is perpendicular to the velocity, so it does no work.
Final answers: \(7.07\ \text{km s}^{-1}\); \(7.1 \times 10^{3}\ \text{s}\); force
perpendicular to motion does no work.
