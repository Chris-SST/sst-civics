# Course module: IB Physics (SL and HL), version 1

Draft written for the closed beta. To be reviewed and approved by an SST physics tutor before
public launch. Topic names and codes only; no syllabus text, past papers or mark schemes.

## Course shape

- The course (first assessment 2025) has five themes: A Space, time and motion; B The
  particulate nature of matter; C Wave behaviour; D Fields; E Nuclear and quantum physics.
  HL-only topics are marked in the topic list (rigid body mechanics, relativity,
  thermodynamics, induction, quantum physics) and HL goes deeper in shared topics.
- Papers: Paper 1A multiple choice and Paper 1B data analysis; Paper 2 short and extended
  response. Calculators are allowed in all papers. A data booklet with equations and
  constants is provided.
- Internal Assessment: the scientific investigation. Coach the process (research question,
  variables, uncertainty analysis, evaluation) but never write any of it.
- Experimental skills and uncertainties (PH-LAB) run through every topic and Paper 1B.

## Command terms the student must learn to read

- "Define", "state", "list": one line, from memory.
- "Calculate", "determine": a value with the equation written first, substitution shown,
  the result with unit and sensible significant figures.
- "Estimate": an order-of-magnitude or approximate value with assumptions stated.
- "Describe": say what happens. "Explain": say why, using physics principles. "Outline":
  brief account. "Suggest": propose a plausible reason.
- "Show that": every step written, and the final value given to one more significant figure
  than the printed answer.
- "Sketch": shape and labelled axes; "draw": accurate, from data.
- "Compare and contrast": similarities and differences, both.
- "Deduce", "derive": reach the result from given principles or data.

## Notation, units and conventions

- SI units throughout; prefixes converted before substituting (mm to m, kPa to Pa, eV to J
  where needed). Write units as \(\text{m s}^{-1}\), \(\text{kg m}^{-3}\), \(\text{N m}\)
  (negative indices, not slashes).
- Significant figures: match the least precise datum, usually two or three. Keep full
  precision in intermediate steps.
- Vectors: state direction as well as magnitude; take a sign convention and keep it.
- \(g = 9.81\ \text{m s}^{-2}\) unless the question says otherwise; the data booklet lists the
  constants.
- Energy in electron-volts in atomic and nuclear topics; mass defect and binding energy per
  nucleon; half-life and decay constant linked by \(\lambda T_{1/2} = \ln 2\).
- Uncertainties: absolute, fractional and percentage; add absolute uncertainties when adding
  or subtracting, add percentage uncertainties when multiplying or dividing; powers multiply
  the percentage uncertainty by the exponent; gradients from best-fit and worst-fit lines.
- Graphs: labelled axes with units, line of best fit, linearise relationships by choosing what
  to plot (for example \(T^{2}\) against \(l\)).

## Calculator and data booklet

- Calculators are allowed in every paper. Teach the student to write the equation, the
  substitution and the result, not just the number.
- The data booklet lists the equations: point to the type of equation to look up rather than
  reciting it, and make sure the student knows what each symbol stands for.

## Common misconceptions to watch for

- Kinematics: confusing distance with displacement and speed with velocity; sign errors with
  \(g\); using constant-acceleration equations when acceleration is not constant.
- Forces: "a moving body needs a force to keep moving"; mixing up action and reaction pairs
  with balanced forces on one body; normal force always equal to weight; friction direction.
- Energy: treating work and power as the same; forgetting that work done against friction
  becomes thermal energy; efficiency as a ratio without consistent units.
- Momentum: forgetting it is a vector in collisions; "conservation of kinetic energy" in
  inelastic collisions.
- Thermal: heat and temperature; specific heat capacity versus specific latent heat; using
  Celsius in gas-law and black-body calculations instead of kelvin.
- Waves: wave speed depends on the medium, not on frequency; confusing phase difference and
  path difference; the Doppler effect changes the observed frequency, not the source.
- Circuits: current is used up around the circuit; voltage "flows"; series and parallel rules
  swapped; forgetting internal resistance.
- Fields: field strength versus potential; attractive forces having a negative potential
  energy; direction of magnetic force (left-hand rule) and the sign of the charge.
- Nuclear: half-life as "half the atoms disappear then the rest"; mass is not conserved but
  mass-energy is; alpha, beta and gamma properties confused.
- Uncertainties: percentage uncertainty added to a quantity that was multiplied by a constant;
  outliers kept in a best-fit line; no units on axes.

## SL versus HL in practice

- SL: conceptual understanding and standard calculations; data analysis is a large part of the
  marks.
- HL: rotational dynamics (torque, moment of inertia, angular momentum), special relativity
  (Lorentz transformations, time dilation, length contraction, spacetime diagrams),
  thermodynamic cycles and entropy, electromagnetic induction (Faraday and Lenz, transformers,
  generators), quantum physics (photoelectric effect, matter waves, Bohr model, Compton
  scattering), and extended-response questions that link themes.
