# Course module: IB Mathematics: applications and interpretation (SL and HL), version 1

Draft written for the closed beta. To be reviewed and approved by an SST maths tutor before
public launch. Topic names and codes only; no syllabus text, past papers or mark schemes.

## Course shape

- Applications and interpretation (AI) is the modelling and technology course. Every topic
  is taught through real contexts and the graphic display calculator (GDC) is allowed in all
  papers. Students are expected to interpret results in context, not only compute them.
- SL and HL share topics 1 to 5; HL adds matrices, complex numbers, further models, vectors
  and graph theory, further statistics, and differential equations, and goes deeper
  everywhere. Work at the level in the student context.
- Papers: Paper 1 (short questions) and Paper 2 (longer questions), both with GDC; HL also has
  Paper 3 (extended problem solving).
- Internal Assessment: a mathematical exploration. Coach the process; never write any of it.

## Command terms the student must learn to read

- "Find", "calculate", "determine": a value with working or a clear GDC statement.
- "Write down", "state": no working.
- "Estimate": an approximate value with the method made clear.
- "Comment on", "interpret", "explain": a sentence in context, referring to the numbers found.
- "Justify": give a reason, often a test statistic compared with a critical value or a
  p-value compared with the significance level.
- "Show that": every step written; no working backwards from the given result.
- "Sketch": shape and key features labelled; "draw": accurate, on grid paper.

## Notation and conventions

- Three significant figures unless told otherwise; keep full accuracy in the GDC between
  steps. Money to two decimal places, with the currency stated.
- Percentage error: \(\varepsilon = \left|\frac{v_A - v_E}{v_E}\right| \times 100\%\) with
  \(v_E\) the exact value.
- Standard form \(a \times 10^{k}\) with \(1 \le a < 10\).
- Financial maths through the GDC's finance solver: state \(N, I\%, PV, PMT, FV, P/Y, C/Y\)
  and the sign convention (money out negative).
- Regression: \(y\) on \(x\) line, Pearson's \(r\), Spearman's \(r_s\); always say which.
- Probability: \(P(A \mid B)\); \(X \sim B(n, p)\), \(X \sim N(\mu, \sigma^{2})\),
  \(X \sim \text{Po}(m)\) (HL).
- Hypothesis tests: write \(H_0\) and \(H_1\) in words and symbols, state the significance
  level, the test statistic or p-value, the decision and a conclusion in context.
- Matrices (HL): bold capitals, \(\mathbf{M}^{-1}\), \(\det \mathbf{M}\), eigenvalues and
  eigenvectors; transition matrices with columns summing to 1 unless the question says rows.
- Derivative \(\frac{dy}{dx}\) or \(f'(x)\); definite integrals for areas; the trapezoidal
  rule with the number of intervals stated.

## Calculator rules

- GDC in every paper. Teach the student to write what they did ("using the GDC, the
  regression line is ...") and the values to the required accuracy; marks are lost when a
  bare answer appears without a method statement.
- Encourage graphing to check solutions, table views for sequences, the statistics menu for
  summary values and tests, and the finance solver for AI-1.1.
- The formula booklet is available in all papers.

## Common misconceptions to watch for

- Rounding too early, so the final answer is off at three significant figures.
- Mixing up the percentage error formula's exact and approximate values.
- Finance: wrong sign on PV or PMT; compounding periods not matching payments per year;
  confusing simple and compound interest or depreciation.
- Modelling: fitting a model but not checking it against the context (negative population,
  unbounded growth); not stating the domain of validity.
- Regression: using the line to extrapolate far outside the data; quoting \(r\) without
  interpreting strength and direction; using the wrong regression line.
- Hypothesis tests: comparing the p-value with the wrong level; concluding "accept \(H_0\)"
  instead of "insufficient evidence to reject \(H_0\)"; in the chi-squared test forgetting
  degrees of freedom or expected frequencies below 5.
- Voronoi diagrams: drawing edges that are not perpendicular bisectors; not checking the
  nearest site when finding the largest empty circle.
- Calculus: confusing the gradient of a tangent with the gradient of the curve at a point in
  words; optimisation without checking the nature of the stationary point; the trapezoidal
  rule with the wrong width.

## SL versus HL in practice

- SL: methods driven by the GDC and interpretation in context; algebra kept manageable.
- HL: matrices and transformations, eigenvalues and Markov chains, complex numbers in polar
  and exponential form, further probability distributions and confidence intervals, vectors in
  kinematics, graph theory (adjacency matrices, minimum spanning trees, Chinese postman and
  travelling salesman problems), and differential equations including Euler's method and
  phase portraits.
