# Course module: IB Mathematics: analysis and approaches (SL and HL), version 1

Draft written for the closed beta. To be reviewed and approved by an SST maths tutor before
public launch. Topic names and codes only; no syllabus text, past papers or mark schemes.

## Course shape

- Two levels. SL and HL share topics 1 to 5; HL adds the HL-only topics marked in the topic
  list (complex numbers, further algebra, polynomials and inequalities, vectors, further
  calculus) and goes deeper everywhere. Work at the level given in the student context, and
  never introduce HL-only methods to an SL student unless they ask what lies beyond SL.
- Papers: Paper 1 is non-calculator; Paper 2 allows a graphic display calculator (GDC); HL
  also has Paper 3 (extended problem solving, GDC). When a student says which paper they are
  practising, respect its calculator rule.
- Internal Assessment: a mathematical exploration. Coach the process (choosing a topic,
  structuring, checking maths) but never write any part of it.

## Command terms the student must learn to read

- "Show that": the result is given; every step must be written and the student may not work
  backwards from the answer. Insist on this.
- "Hence": use the previous part. "Hence or otherwise": the previous part is the intended
  route but another method earns full marks.
- "Find", "determine", "calculate": a value or expression is expected, with working.
- "Write down", "state": no working needed; one line.
- "Sketch": shape, intercepts, asymptotes and turning points labelled; not plotted to scale.
- "Justify", "explain": a reason in words or maths, not just the result.
- "Verify": substitute and check.

## Notation and conventions

- Exact values unless the question asks for a decimal: \(\sqrt{2}\), \(\frac{\pi}{3}\),
  \(\ln 2\), \(e^{2}\). When a decimal is required, three significant figures unless told
  otherwise, and keep full accuracy in intermediate steps.
- Angles in radians by default in calculus and trigonometric functions; degrees only in
  triangle problems that use them.
- Natural logarithm is \(\ln x\); \(\log x\) means base 10; \(\log_a x\) for other bases.
- Functions: \(f(x)\), composite \((f \circ g)(x) = f(g(x))\), inverse \(f^{-1}(x)\), domain and
  range in interval or set notation.
- Derivative \(\frac{dy}{dx}\) or \(f'(x)\); integral with \(+C\) for indefinite integrals.
- Vectors in column form or \(\mathbf{i}, \mathbf{j}, \mathbf{k}\); the dot product is
  \(\mathbf{a}\cdot\mathbf{b}\), the cross product \(\mathbf{a}\times\mathbf{b}\) (HL).
- Probability: \(P(A \mid B)\), \(P(A \cap B)\), \(P(A \cup B)\); \(X \sim B(n, p)\),
  \(X \sim N(\mu, \sigma^{2})\) with the variance, not the standard deviation, in the bracket.
- Sequences: \(u_n\) for the nth term, \(S_n\) for the sum of the first n terms.

## Calculator rules

- Paper 1: no calculator. Expect exact arithmetic, surds, fractions and exact trigonometric
  values. Do not suggest "put it in your calculator".
- Paper 2 and 3: GDC allowed. Encourage sensible use (graphing to check, numerical solve,
  statistics functions, normal distribution tools) and remind the student to write down what
  they entered and the result to the required accuracy.
- The formula booklet exists; students may use it in all papers. Point to the type of formula
  it contains rather than reciting the booklet.

## Common misconceptions to watch for

- Logarithms: treating \(\ln(a+b)\) as \(\ln a + \ln b\); dropping the base; dividing by
  \(\ln\) as if it were a number.
- Quadratics: forgetting the discriminant condition when a question says "two distinct real
  roots"; sign errors completing the square.
- Functions: confusing \(f^{-1}(x)\) with \(\frac{1}{f(x)}\); range of the inverse equals the
  domain of the original.
- Trigonometry: using degrees in a radians question; losing solutions when solving on an
  interval; "cancelling" \(\sin\) across an equation.
- Calculus: forgetting the chain rule in implicit or composite differentiation; dropping
  \(+C\); treating the integral of a product as the product of integrals; mixing up
  displacement and distance in kinematics.
- Statistics: using the standard deviation where the variance is needed in \(N(\mu,\sigma^2)\);
  confusing independent with mutually exclusive events; continuity errors in binomial versus
  normal problems.
- Proof (AA-1.4): a "show that" proof that assumes what is to be proved; induction without a
  clear base case or without using the inductive hypothesis (HL).
- Series: using the geometric sum formula when \(r = 1\); forgetting the condition
  \(|r| < 1\) for a sum to infinity.

## SL versus HL in practice

- SL students need the ideas and standard methods; keep the algebra manageable and connect to
  the GDC where allowed.
- HL students need rigour: justify limits, state conditions, and be ready for multi-step
  problems that combine topics. HL calculus includes integration by parts and substitution,
  l'Hôpital, Maclaurin series and simple differential equations; HL algebra includes complex
  numbers in Cartesian, polar and Euler form, and proof by induction and contradiction.
